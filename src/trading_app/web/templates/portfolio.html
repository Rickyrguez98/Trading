How the forecast columns are made. For each stock you hold, 2,000 possible futures are simulated from its own daily moves of the last three years. The stock's past trend is kept only in proportion to how statistically reliable it is (a weak trend counts for nothing).
  • Prognosis: the overall verdict. "Waiting looks worthwhile" or "likely to fall" appears only when a trend has passed its back-test (✓); otherwise it says there is no reliable direction and shows which way the unproven model leans. It gives the expected price and the date for it, when the middle scenario gets back to your break-even price (for lots at a loss), and the chance the price halves within a year.
  • Trend: up when at least 55% of the futures end above today's price, down when at most 45% do. The small print is its track record over up to ten years of monthly tests. ✓ appears only when that record beats a coin flip with 95% confidence, counting overlapping tests only as the independent evidence they really are. "Needs N yrs" means the stock does not have the years of prices that horizon needs to be judged (a 1-year call needs about ten).
  • Expected: the middle outcome (median) at that horizon. The arithmetic average is in the CSV; for volatile stocks it is pulled far up by a few extreme futures. Min / Max: the average lowest and highest close along the way.
  • Short-term recommendation: sixty sell prices are tried over the next month, each scored by its improvement over selling today times its chance of being reached; the best one reached in at least a quarter of the futures is the limit to place. The stop is where the price falls in about one month in ten. A trend is used here only if it has a ✓ at one month; otherwise these levels come from volatility alone. If no limit adds at least 0.5% over today's price, it says sell now. Percentages compare with what that lot cost you, after commission.
Closing prices only (intraday swings are wider) and a stop is assumed to fill at its price. These are statistical estimates from past prices, not a prediction you can rely on and not financial advice.
