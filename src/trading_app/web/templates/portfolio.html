How the forecast columns are made. For each stock you hold, 2,000 possible futures are simulated from its own daily moves of the last three years. The stock's past trend is kept only in proportion to how statistically reliable it is (a weak trend counts for nothing).
  • Trend: up when at least 55% of the futures end above today's price, down when at most 45% do. The small print is its track record: how many past test periods it called the direction right. ✓ appears only when that record beats a coin flip with 95% confidence; "too few tests" means the history is too short to judge (always the case for 6 months and 1 year).
  • Expected: the middle outcome (median) at that horizon. The arithmetic average is in the CSV; for volatile stocks it is pulled far up by a few extreme futures. Min / Max: the average lowest and highest close along the way.
  • Short-term recommendation: sixty sell prices are tried over the next month, each scored by its improvement over selling today times its chance of being reached; the best one reached in at least a quarter of the futures is the limit to place. The stop is where the price falls in about one month in ten. A trend is used here only if it has a ✓ at one month; none does today, so these levels come from volatility alone. If no limit adds at least 0.5% over today's price, it says sell now. Percentages compare with what that lot cost you, after commission.
Closing prices only (intraday swings are wider) and a stop is assumed to fill at its price. These are statistical estimates from past prices, not a prediction you can rely on and not financial advice.
